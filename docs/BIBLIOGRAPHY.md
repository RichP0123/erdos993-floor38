# Bibliography and source roles

Checked 28 September 2026 against the primary links and saved local records listed here. This is a focused provenance bibliography, not an exhaustive literature review or proof of novelty. No title is evidence that this project used the author's source code. Machine-readable entries are in references.bib.

1. **Y. Alavi, P. J. Malde, A. J. Schwenk and P. Erdős.** *The Vertex Independence Sequence of a Graph Is Not Constrained*. Congressus Numerantium 58 (1987), 15–23. [Original paper](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1987-33.pdf). Problem 3, printed page 21, explicitly raises the tree/forest unimodality question. The original also notes that arbitrary unimodal products need not be unimodal.

2. **Brett Reynolds.** *Mean bounds, structural reductions, and exhaustive verification for tree independence polynomial unimodality*. [Zenodo record 19100781](https://zenodo.org/records/19100781). For the historical later census used in this project's audit, cite separately the [August census directory at commit b85d5dc01bdcaf3d6853147b28c350d74d3ede21](https://github.com/BrettRey/erdos-problem-993/tree/b85d5dc01bdcaf3d6853147b28c350d74d3ede21/results/lc_census_20260814). Its pinned records cover orders 27–32, with 64 shards per order and zero unimodality alarms. The earlier article/README advertises coverage only through 29. The later records were audited as historical computational evidence; the billion-tree sweeps were not rerun here and no Lean census theorem is attributed to them.

3. **SciNet / scinet-ai contributors.** *Erdős #993 forest computation*, directory `erdos-993-forests` in `math-number-theory`, commit `fafb35784d4235c9e5dd701fd3b2c1f4955ae9ec`. [Pinned primary repository](https://github.com/scinet-ai/math-number-theory/tree/fafb35784d4235c9e5dd701fd3b2c1f4955ae9ec/erdos-993-forests). Cite for historical forest computation/closure. The descriptive title here does not repeat the README's priority claim or invent a named human author.

4. **Erdős 993 project, FLOOR208 research round.** *FLOOR208 — complete finite-difference box exclusion*. Internal report and reproduction materials, with recorded local acquisition and complete certificate replay on 15 September 2026. The saved project prompt, matching returns and intake/replay audits establish the commissioned project-round origin. The individual producing seat/model is not recorded; no human author or public DOI is invented. The original report and programs are not separately redistributed with the Lean sources. See [the attribution ledger](ATTRIBUTION.md).

5. **J. Keilson and H. Gerber.** *Some Results for Discrete Unimodality*. Journal of the American Statistical Association 66(334) (1971), 386–389. [Publisher/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1971.10482273). Publisher metadata and abstract checked. The website's 2012 online date is not the original publication year. The original full text was not obtained in this review.

6. **Bruce E. Sagan.** *Compositions inside a rectangle and unimodality*. Author-hosted manuscript dated 5 May 2008. [Primary PDF](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/bsagan/papers/old/cir.pdf). Proposition 2.1 states the polynomial strong-unimodality characterization and credits Keilson–Gerber. Used to verify the precise finite-support interpretation; not represented as the original source of the principle.

7. **S. G. Hoggar.** *Chromatic polynomials and logarithmic concavity*. Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B 16(3) (1974), 248–254. [Publisher/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0095-8956%2874%2990071-9). Classical log-concavity background; distinct from the required unimodal-times-log-concave statement.

8. **Leonardo de Moura and Sebastian Ullrich.** *The Lean 4 Theorem Prover and Programming Language (System Description)* (2021). [Authors' system paper](https://lean-lang.org/papers/lean4.pdf). Cite alongside the exact version/revision, rather than suggesting the paper identifies the local binary.

9. **The mathlib Community.** *The Lean mathematical library*. [arXiv:1910.09336](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09336), 2019; related publication [DOI 10.1145/3372885.3373824](https://doi.org/10.1145/3372885.3373824). Software used here: Mathlib revision `c5ea00351c28e24afc9f0f84379aa41082b1188f` with other packages pinned by the release lock.

10. **Tong Zhang and Wei Li.** *Unimodality of Forest Independence Polynomials*. Zenodo, version 27 September 2026. [DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22999166](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22999166). On 28 September 2026, the title, authors, issue date, version and active/findable DOI were verified in the [DataCite record](https://api.datacite.org/dois/10.5281/zenodo.22999166); direct Zenodo landing-page access returned HTTP 403 in this environment. This resolves the earlier bibliographic retrieval uncertainty, not verification of the paper's general theorem or companion computations. Its Proposition 1.2 states unimodality for forests through 60; the present theorem establishes the same conclusion through 37 by an independent method.

11. **Ethan X. Fang, Junwei Lu, Eran Nevo, Yuan Yao and Hailun Zheng.** *Unimodality of Independence Polynomials for Sufficiently Large Forests*. [arXiv:2609.20961v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20961v1), submitted 17 September 2026. This concerns sufficiently large forests. The new local interface lemmas also match the counting and unimodality formulations inspected in the authors' [Basic.lean](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/junwei-lu/Erdos_993_Tree_Independent_Set_Unimodality/main/ErdosProblem993/Basic.lean). Their project was not built or audited here; the interface comparison is not a combined proof of every order.
